And So The Gods of Circularity Smiled Down Upon The Earth

Prelude.

The wyrm feels

a hunger

that spans

from the points of its fangs

to the tip of its tail.

It squirms around

blindly, searching for

something to plug up its throat.

And then,

its snout meets a

solid.

Jaws expand, teeth bared

as it swallows

something thin, scaly, long.

Slitted eyes blink

at the length of its body

looped back around

to itself.

I.

Let me begin with a story.

There once was a sparrow / in a society of sparrows.

In feather and breezes, they lived / quite pleasant lives.

Food / was never a concern; / there were mealworms abound / to goad into their beaks.

And so / life was good.

But a sparrow, the sparrow, / our sparrow, in a society of sparrows, / felt a distinct emptiness.

Yes, an emptiness, the kind that starts as / just a pinprick in the heart / but feeds on all around it, / consuming the organs until / there was nothing left but the hole.

It sought out guidance, / but it was met with / reassurances that every creature / held such an emptiness / within their breast.

Even the mealworms, simple as they were, / held a hole– / that was why / they could be tempted into beaks / and swallowed up.

But still / this left our sparrow befuddled.

Surely, / there had to be a way to rid of the emptiness?

Shelter and food and societies / could do nothing but distract, / and in the next moment, / the emptiness would rear its head / and forcibly steal the focus / of our sparrow’s thoughts.

Day and night passed, / and our sparrow did as sparrows do: / eat, preen, sleep– / eat, preen, sleep– / eat, preen, sleep.

Our sparrow was distraught / and felt nothing at all / but the emptiness within.

Driven to desperation, / it wandered away at night / feet and wings guiding it in circles.

Eat, preen, sleep– / but food had lost all its meaning and taste– / there was little purpose in preserving feathers– / and its bare feet guided it away in the depths of night.

Day, night, day, night, / until it came upon a creature– / a beautiful creature, / with paws dipped in ebony tar, / a pelt that had stolen the color from fields of russet, / and snow that clung to the tip of its bushy tail, its muzzle, its underbelly.

Its breath was rank with the scent of rotting flesh, / and yet the manner in which the sun shone off those / pearly stalactites and stalagmites / only added to its allure.

Our sparrow was curious, / and so conversed with the fair creature.

“I have felt an emptiness,” confessed our sparrow, / “and day by day it has only grown / to eat up the rest of me– / heart and lungs–liver and intestines–throat and tongue.

I fear that it will eat me / until nothing remains.”

And so the creature replied, / “I have felt likewise, / but I’ve an idea.

If you leap into my mouth, / perhaps we can fill our holes / with companionship.”

And our desperate, little sparrow / felt overjoyed in this shared moment, / thanked the fiery creature quite profusely, / as was always taught of it, and leapt into its maw.

And in the next moment, / before our sparrow could even think to be anything / but ecstatic, / the cave closed in, / and nothing was left of our sparrow / but a bloody pulp that slid down the fair creature’s throat, / tasting like dew drops after a drought.

The other sparrows would leer and mock, / asking how one was so foolish as to not know better, / to never trust beautiful creatures.

But they do not understand / that the beautiful creature had not lied, / and that it, too, would be tempted into the claws / of a brown beast, far larger than it was.

That beast, too, had a hole / that would grow and grow and grow until / it left nothing but a corpse.

Then the mealworms, in their insatiable emptiness, / would swarm the body / before finding their way / into beaks.

And so it goes.

II.

Let us transition, now, to an idea.

There are a thousand somethings in the world,but only one type of nothing.
Nothing existsas a contrast to something:

the space between the stars,

the hole formed from a rip in one’s jeans–

the gaping emptiness from within,

the space enclosed within a straw.

Together, they forma yin and yang,

two opposing forces

forever contrasting one another.

But that isn’t right,is it?
Because nothing is definedas an absence of something–

but an absence is still a thing.

Definitions can only applyto an existing idea–

something in existence.

So nothingness and somethingare almost the same, in a way.
Yet the contraststill stands:

same and different–

everything wrapped into one,

fingers interlocked, palms pressed flush against one another,

a sweat coating skin, reminders of humanity,

and in between sits a black hole,

warping something and nothing

in tandem.

And soit goes:
something,then nothing, then something,then nothing

over and over

until it transforms

into something and something and something, something, something.

Something and something,following each other,

contrasting each other, growing from each other,

tearing away from each other.

Somethingand nothingand nothingand something

falling, spiraling, twirling

through the air like ribbons–

shadow and light forming a dichotomy

over and over and over and over and over–!

It’s rings of somethingall the way down.

III.

Let me describe to you an image.

The sky is hazy with clouds,

orange and purple and blue

hues color it, wisping around

that great ball of light

we call the sun.

Beneath it there lies

greens of every shade

stacked atop a rough brown–

that of tree trunks or rich soil–

and even further sits a thick rush

of frothy, white water.

Aqua slithers

past rocks, down cliffs, across mud,

dragging along silt and salmon.

Yes, salmon–

those orange-fleshed fish with their hooked jaws,

tasting the water that was once briney and now clear.

From here they had come–

to the blues of the oceans they fled–

and back they return,

stomachs filled with spawn.

In droves they press upstream,

marking the whites of the river

with the silver glint of scales;

some might have sworn that

there were more fish than water.

Journeying upward has perils abound–

creatures far more vicious and hungry,

waterfalls that they must climb,

and ultimately,

they will reach their final resting place

after the birth of their children.

Yet still,

they travel.

Perhaps the salmon are musing–

thinking to themself about

what future will come of their children

and not for themself–

they know that

which awaits them at their trails’ end.

And despite it all,

none of them will ever

dare to turn back.

Thus, they go

forward, forward, forward,

and when their children are born,

they will be greeted with the decaying flesh

of the previous generation.

And those children will shift

from river to ocean,

calling the salt and brine their home

until they too return

to their birthing place and tomb

to welcome their own spawn.

IV.

Let me finish by sharing a feeling.

An energy resides

low in the spine,

resting right at the base,

coiled like a spring.

It sits and waits,

and waits and waits and waits,

a string pulled tight until

release.

When the moment comes,

the body need not think,

for the energy shoots up the spine,

zipping through webs of nerves

and granting life to muscle.

Pushes a leg to kick,

jerking away an arm,

jumping back

at the slightest touch of pain.

This energy shoots outward

in hopes of survival,

and once it is done,

it creates a halo about the head

before dissipating into air,

while a new energy builds

and builds and builds,

ready to be fired

once again.

Each time a struggle

to keep nerves firing

in order to fire more nerves,

fighting again and again for life

until those electric impulses

dull and fizzle away.

Life for the sake of being alive–

to live and live and live again.

To breathe in cool air

and feel the sands falling between our fingers

and taste the prayers on our tongue

for more time, more time, more time.

We are alive, asleep, dead, conscious,

a thousand things,

and yet we strive

for life

through shots of electricity

which will fire

again and again and again.

Life for life for life

expressed through those involuntary motions,

the movement of impulses

to jerk away from danger.

Postlude.

The wyrm, now a ring,

can do nothing but

slither in circles,

feeling the weight of its own scales

upon its tongue.

And when it peers upward,

its gaze is met with

a thousand other wyrms,

choking on their own, thin bodies,

forming yet another circle,

revolving about nothing.

And if the wyrm could see more,

see further, see beyond,

it would see

an infinite number of wyrm-rings,

posited in such a way that

it formed a bigger circle,

which formed yet another, larger circle,

and another,

and another,

forever.

And each circle

was almost its own wyrm,

chasing the tip of its tail,

through the nothingness of space.
